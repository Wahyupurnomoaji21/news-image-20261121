# News Image — 21 November 2026

## The Night Lima Stepped Outside

One photograph, one day. On Friday evening, 20 November 2026, a
magnitude 6.8 earthquake struck the Andean region of Junin in
central Peru — 7:09 pm local time, about 9 km northeast of
Carhuamayo, 108–129 km deep — and the tremor rolled 320 km to the
capital. In Lima, residents poured out of their homes and into the
avenues; television footage showed lawmakers leaving Congress.
No casualties or major damage were reported, and there was no
tsunami threat — but for one evening, a city of ten million stood
outside, looking up at the buildings it had just fled.

The image shows that minute: a mother holding her child wrapped
in a blanket, an elderly woman steadied by her son, neighbours
holding each other in a Lima avenue at dusk, taxis stopped with
their headlights on and emergency lights flashing down the street,
the Andes behind the towers. The masthead names the day it sums
up — Saturday 21 November 2026 — because the news of the day
belongs to the morning it was first reported.

## How it works

`main.py` copies `entry.png` byte-for-byte into the directory given
by `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` (default `/output`). It refuses to write if
the image exceeds 1 MiB, then verifies the copy by file size and
SHA-256 and prints the hash. No network access, no randomness, no
rendering at run time — the image is a fixed asset in this repo.

## Why this moment

Three stories led 21 November 2026: the Peru earthquake, Russian
missiles coming down in Poland in a grave NATO airspace violation,
and the S&P 500 sliding into correction as AI shares sold off.
The earthquake leads because it is the day's freshest human story
— the ground moving under a capital city at dusk, ten million
people stepping outside together — and because it is the one a
single photograph can carry whole: no chart, no crater, no
communique, just a street full of people looking up.
